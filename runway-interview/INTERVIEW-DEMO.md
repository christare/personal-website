# Last Shuttle: interview demo

Aim for two minutes. Keep the game open before the call. Dialogue waits for you; click NEXT or press Space. A first click reveals a typing line, and the next advances. Turn sound on before sharing and keep it quiet enough to speak over.

## Opening, 15 seconds

“I made a playable sci-fi story with six Runway shots. Your friend is trapped on an infected station. You can leave him, release him, or investigate a safer way out. I wanted to turn generated imagery into something an audience participates in.”

## Play, about 75 seconds

Enter your first name → BEGIN → ANSWER THE EMERGENCY CALL → GO BACK FOR ALEX → READ THE INCIDENT LOG → USE THE SEALED CAPSULE → LAUNCH WITH ALEX → CONTINUE.

Move through the individual dialogue beats between decisions. Let the door, lab feed, and capsule shots breathe for a few seconds. You can talk over a scene without losing a line.

At the incident log: “This is the key choice. The information changes what you can do. The same small set of environments supports three endings.”

At the capsule: “The generated shot already had the capsule sealed and moving on a rail, so I wrote the transfer around that. I adapted the story to the footage instead of describing an action the viewer couldn't see.”

## Educator and content lead payoff, 30 seconds

“The tutorial would start with this playable result, then work backward: one reference image for the visual language, a six-shot list, animation, and branching interaction. I'd show a mismatch and the decision that fixed it, not just a sequence of perfect outputs.”

“For YouTube, I'd make one complete build tutorial, cut the strongest decision into a Short, and invite viewers to share which ending they found. I'd judge the lesson by whether viewers can reproduce the workflow, alongside retention and clicks through to the game.”

Suggested video title: “I Made a Playable Sci-Fi Story With 6 AI Shots.”

## If asked how it was made

“Runway supplied the original motion shots. OpenAI generated the additional stills and exact references for environmental payoff animations. I directed the concept, selected the footage, and shaped the experience using AI coding tools.”

The interface, branching logic, text, and synthesized sound run in the browser. The game makes no live generation calls. Do not claim you manually coded everything or quote generation costs or model settings you have not verified in your Runway history.

## Backup

Use the local game at http://localhost:3000/last-shuttle if the public site is not available. Start it with `npm run dev` from the personal-website folder. Keep a short screen recording of the rescue route available before the interview, using your normal recording setup.

## Visual direction

No people, faces, hands, silhouettes or designed creature. Alex stays unseen; two coffee mugs, a packed bag, an empty seat and the radio establish the relationship. The tightened cut uses only three emotional stills: shared coffee, the empty seat, and the isolation bay. Dialogue stays over the six original motion environments. Three additional payoff stills are integrated; their Runway animations are pending. Use PAYOFF-SHOTS.md for the exact input images, motion prompts and export filenames.
