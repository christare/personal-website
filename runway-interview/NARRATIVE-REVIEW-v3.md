# Last Shuttle — selective feedback pass, V3

Read with [the complete V3 screenplay](LAST-SHUTTLE-SCREENPLAY-v3.md). V2 and BASELINE V1 remain preserved. This is a small revision to the existing story: seven replacement lines, two new player responses on existing images, and later presentation of ending labels. No new footage, sound, route, outcome or infection rule was added.

## What was useful

The strongest diagnosis was that Alex kept asking for help in the same register. Shortening every plea would not solve that. The revised exchanges give Alex different ways of dealing with the player:

- **A practical problem:** “I'm stuck in the lab. The reader won't take my card.” Alex has tried something concrete; the sentence leaves room for irritation before fear.
- **A shared object:** “My bag's already on your shuttle, {name}. Don't leave without me.” This recalls the memory without naming the promise, and gives the empty seat a connection to the present conversation.
- **An instruction:** “You're there. Okay. The release is on your side.” Reaching the door changes Alex's tactic from pleading at a distance to directing the player.
- **Pressure:** “I know what the light means. Just open it.” Alex understands the warning and presses anyway. There is no invented false-positive explanation.
- **An interrupted excuse:** “I saw the result. I was going to tell you, I just—” / **YOU:** “You were going to let me open it.” The player answers the betrayal rather than moving straight into transfer instructions. No unsupported timeline is added.

The early-departure plea loses the generic “I need you,” keeping the existing physical threat and the direct request to come back. The lab camera line loses the redundant question, leaving “Tell me you're seeing this.” These trims preserve the information and pressure.

The open-door route gains **YOU: “Sit down. I've got you.”** after Alex's evasive promise to explain. The unchanged air warning follows the player's attempt to offer comfort. This gives compassion a moment to exist before its consequence arrives.

Ending labels, completion status, color grading, credits and replay now wait for the player to continue past the final line. The last image and caption stay visible. This follows the game's existing reading controls and gives the player time to read, without a forced blackout or timer.

## What stayed

The ordinary coffee memory and original promise remain. So do Alex's “Okay. Just don't hang up,” both abandonment captions, “You hold your breath. It doesn't help,” and the final “Of course.” Each already has a clear function; swapping in a more emphatic line would risk explaining its meaning.

The brief narration getting Alex aboard remains necessary because that action happens off screen. A pressure-stable system message would establish the airlock state, but would not establish that Alex reached the shuttle. Added radio static would also break the current production rule that stills introduce no new audio.

## What the feedback overstates

**The capsule remains the safer rescue option.** The new response to Alex's lie gives the player a personal reason to hesitate; it does not make the physical options equally costly. That is acceptable for this demo. The informed-abandonment route asks whether fear and betrayal outweigh a possible rescue. The early exit asks whether the player will even investigate. They are different decisions, without needing to be evenly balanced.

The proposed power diversion, lethal cold and freezing window would require a new causal setup and audiovisual support. It would be a plot revision, not a surgical improvement. The sealed capsule's existing movement already gives the rescue route a frightening test of its barrier. Alex's lock question stays; this pass does not invent a capsule that can never be reopened or a medical authority with sinister intentions.

Military call signs, “souls aboard,” a centrifuge, and a test result from three days ago all add a register or a fact the story has not earned. A longer breakfast joke would slow the opening and weaken its ordinary quality. None was adopted.

## Remaining narrative limit

The story is concise and readable, but the relationship still depends partly on the player supplying their own history with Alex. That is intentional under the no-people rule. If expanding later, the most useful addition would be another specific shared behavior that pays off in an existing choice, not more lore or generalized pleading. For the current interview demo, stop here and let the footage and the decisions carry it.
