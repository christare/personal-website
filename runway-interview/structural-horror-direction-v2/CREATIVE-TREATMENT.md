## The corrected brief

The first impression must be an extraordinary moving image with a clear dramatic consequence. Runway is central to the work. A technically reliable animation of a guard or a chair does not fulfill that brief. The experience should demonstrate visual ambition, a coherent fictional rule, deliberate editing and consequential choices.

Retire the corridor-panel and override-guard generation pack. Retire the chair movement as a principal scare. Do not spend Runway credits on those references. Preserve the files only as development history.

Continue to show no people, faces, silhouettes or designed creature. The absence of a visible creature is not a limit on intensity. Materials can undergo extreme, frightening transformations: enormous weight, force, speed, rupture and impossible compression.

## The new physical rule

The contamination behaves like a failure of space confined by airtight boundaries. When a contaminated compartment connects to a fresh volume, it can pull that adjoining volume through the opening, however small the opening is. Machinery, floors and structural members follow the air and compact into impossible density. Intact seals interrupt that connection.

This is a fictional anomalous process, not a claim about ordinary decompression or a realistic airborne pathogen. The dialogue should not deliver its cosmology. Show the effect, demonstrate that a seal interrupts it, and let the player understand what opening Alex's room risks.

The story must stop treating the condition as an ordinary positive infection test. A report can identify a failed containment boundary and unsafe shared air. It cannot explain away the central impossibility with a paragraph of pseudoscience.

The same rule applies to the lab door, shuttle ventilation and sealed transfer. It gives the film a recurring visual language and makes its extreme transformations causal. A new visual is justified by what it does to a boundary, a person’s prospects, or a decision.

## Three major sequences

### 1. An entire room goes through a door

A fixed remote view establishes an empty equipment lab. A door opens a few centimetres. The floor peels from its supports in rigid slabs. A massive workbench folds across its own length. Still-lit fluorescent fixtures travel with their twisted mountings. Everything converges on that small opening and compresses through it in layers far thinner than the original material could occupy.

The last heavy section goes through with shocking speed. A boundary closes and the movement stops. This final closure is necessary to teach that containment matters; the event must not simply end for no reason.

This is the opening spectacle, inside the first ten seconds after starting. It replaces the moving-chair demonstration and much of the report's explanatory burden. It is a recorded failure in a neighboring test compartment, not a view of Alex being physically crushed. Alex remains in a separate sealed room. That geography must be clear without a lore dump.

The supplied `01-lab-compression-concept.png` is an OpenAI concept of the catastrophic midpoint. It establishes material behavior and the narrowing direction of force. It is not a quiet starting frame or a Runway export. A final production pair needs the same room intact with the rear door shut, followed by this compression and a clearly staged closure. Prove the major action in motion before commissioning the rest of the replacement coverage.

### 2. You take Alex aboard and the shuttle becomes the next room

The open-door route carries the contamination into the shuttle. Give the player a short, credible moment of relief and an ordinary exchange with Alex. A familiar vent then becomes the point toward which the cabin is being pulled.

The grille is a beginning, not the whole effect. Console housings tear from their mounts. Deck plates lift in a connected path. The cabin compresses toward an opening that could not fit even a chair. The window remains briefly recognizable, with stars beyond it, so the audience sees the scale of the impossible change. Cut out at the decisive collapse instead of following it with an explanatory medical subtitle.

This replaces the current warning-plus-small-vent-payoff sequence. It should be the visual consequence of breaking quarantine, not a unrelated jump scare. Preserve rigid mechanical materials and an obvious destination for the force. Do not substitute a glowing vortex, a portal, floating debris, or organic tendrils.

### 3. The sealed capsule is the one volume that holds

On the careful rescue route, the capsule travels inside an enclosed transfer passage. The empty bay outside that passage undergoes the same compression. Floors peel toward the breach; fixtures and supports fold past the intact transfer enclosure. The capsule stays readable as the object carrying Alex while the surroundings disappear.

The staging must visibly preserve a continuous sealed route into the shuttle. Do not ask the audience to accept an airtight rescue while showing an open gap around it. The existing capsule impact may become a brief supporting insert if it fits this larger event. It is no longer the sequence's main spectacle.

The final intact capsule is the consequence of choosing containment. After the scale and violence of the transfer, a still or restrained shot of that container can be powerful. It is carrying someone the player cares about, and the player has just learned why it cannot be opened casually.

## The emotional revision

Alex remains someone with a shared history and ordinary plans with the player. Keep the coffee and bag as concrete traces of that relationship, but fold the memory into the existing opening rather than make the audience sit through a separate introductory lesson in friendship.

Alex knows more about the event than they initially admit. Their requests should become specific, evasive and occasionally unfair under pressure. Preserve the player's right to be angry. Neither person should narrate the theme or explain feelings the preceding action already made evident.

The new question is whether the player will risk carrying this phenomenon off the station to save Alex. Rescue should not end with a clean medical handoff and a solved infection. In this treatment, the carrier refuses docking even with the capsule: the player has saved Alex from the station but must remain with them in isolation aboard the shuttle. The seal holds; a cure and a return home are unresolved. The final exchange can be as simple as checking that the other person is still on the channel.

This is a deliberate plot change from the deployed script. Do not quietly keep the old medical-acceptance lines, airborne-test report or tidy recovery designation beneath the new footage.

## Proposed progression

1. Start and enter a name; the remote compartment failure supplies the immediate visual hook.
2. Establish the ordinary relationship briefly, then Alex's live request. The player can leave before investigating, retaining the distinct early-abandonment path.
3. At Alex's sealed room, choose direct release or investigation. The opening sequence has made this a physical risk the player understands.
4. Direct release leads to a short shared escape and the catastrophic shuttle sequence.
5. Investigation exposes Alex's concealment and identifies sealed transfer. The player can leave knowingly or bring Alex aboard inside the capsule.
6. Sealed transfer delivers the major rescue sequence, followed by the cost of remaining isolated together.

There can still be four routes and three ending categories. The ending labels should arrive after the emotional scene, as they do in the current implementation. No extra timer, inventory puzzle or mandatory sequence of minor scares is needed.

## Editing and production priorities

The target remains a compact roughly two-minute experience, with actual timing judged from the final footage. Allocate screen time to actions changing the situation. Use fewer individual reading stops, let short exchanges play across continuous footage, and protect the main action from text covering it. At a genuine decision, stop and give the player readable choices.

Quiet images still have a function: they let the player recognize what is about to be destroyed and feel who is missing afterward. The production mistake was giving ordinary hardware movement the role of a major visual event.

Existing footage is reusable where it provides geography, departure, a sealed object, or a quiet aftermath. It should not dictate a weaker concept merely because it has already been generated. The largest new generation should be attempted first, so the rewrite develops around a visual result that actually works.

Do not promise that Runway will solve every moving part in one generation. Establish the difficult visual result with a strong reference, generate the essential action, then edit a small number of purposeful shots if needed. The interview should show the actual reference-to-motion result and explain one concrete directing decision, with accurate credits: OpenAI reference artwork, Runway animation, Chris Jereza's direction and interactive assembly.
